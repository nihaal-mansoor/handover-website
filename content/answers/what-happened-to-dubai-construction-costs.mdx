Dubai's Construction Cost Index sat at **110.84** at the end of 2025, against a 2019
base of 100. Seven years of building had added about **10%** to the cost of putting
up a residential building.

By June 2026 the same index read **123.05**.

That is **8.8% in six months**, against 10.2% in the preceding seven years. The
second quarter of 2026 alone added **5.9%**, which is more than any full year in the
series.

## The series

Residential buildings, general index, 2019 = 100.

| Period | Index | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2019 Q1 | 100.60 | base |
| 2020 Q4 | 98.27 | below base |
| 2022 Q4 | 103.65 | |
| 2024 Q4 | 107.88 | |
| 2025 Q4 | 110.84 | +10.2% over seven years |
| 2026 Q1 | 113.87 | +2.7% in one quarter |
| 2026 Q2 | 120.61 | +5.9% in one quarter |

The monthly series shows where it happened. January 113.1, February 113.75, March
114.76, April 117.03, then **May 121.76** and June 123.05. Most of the move lands in
a single month.

## It is the shell, not the fit-out

The interesting part is which trades moved. Since 2019:

| Building stage | Change since 2019 |
| --- | --- |
| Block works | +46.2% |
| Super structure | +40.7% |
| Concrete works, substructure and above | +37.9% |
| Foundations | +36.0% |
| Electrical works | +32.6% |
| Air conditioning | +30.0% |
| Finishing work (all) | +21.0% |
| Floor and wall tiles | +9.8% |
| Wages of workers and others | +9.1% |

Structural work has risen roughly four to five times as fast as labour and as the
finishes buyers actually look at.

The material price series says the same thing. Hollow blocks are up around 50%,
ready-mix concrete about 30%, cement about 29% and aggregates about 29%. Meanwhile
gypsum has roughly halved and reinforcing mesh is down more than 20%.

So the expensive part of a building is now the part nobody photographs.

## Why this matters if you are buying

**It squeezes projects sold before it happened.** An off-plan project priced in 2023
or 2024 and built through 2026 was costed against a very different index. That
pressure lands on the developer's margin, and margin pressure is what turns into
specification changes and slipped handover dates. It is not a reason to panic; it is
a reason to check your developer's delivery record and your contract's completion
clause rather than assuming the anticipated date.

**It changes what "cheap" means.** If replacing a building costs materially more
than it did eighteen months ago, a completed building is a different proposition
against a new one than it was. That is an argument about replacement cost, not a
forecast about prices.

**It feeds service charges eventually.** Maintenance, repairs and replacement all
track the same materials. Charges tend to move a year or two behind, not
immediately.

## What this data does not tell you

Being clear about the limits, because this is one index and the jump is unusual:

- **An index is not a price.** It measures the cost of building, not what anything
  sells for. Costs and prices move independently and often in opposite directions.
- **A 4% single-month move is exceptional** and worth watching for revision in later
  releases. The quarterly and monthly files agree, but they are published by the
  same body and may share underlying inputs.
- **It says nothing about where prices go next.** We do not forecast, and anyone
  reading a construction index as a price prediction is over-reading it.
- **Two stages look like placeholders.** Lift works records no change at all since
  2019, and communications is down more than 30%, both of which suggest imputed
  rather than observed data. Treat those lines with suspicion.

## Method

Figures are from the Dubai Statistics Center Construction Cost Index and its average
construction material prices series, published August 2026, unmodified.

Every number above is derived by a script kept with the site, so the arithmetic can
be checked rather than taken on trust. Index levels are the residential general
index on a 2019 base; material comparisons run from the first to the latest
observation in the published series, which for materials begins earlier than 2019.
