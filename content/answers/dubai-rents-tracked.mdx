This page tracks Dubai's official Residential Rent Price Index and is updated each
time a new month is published.

At the latest reading, **July 2026**, the general index stood at **115.45** against a
2024 base of 100. Rents are about **15%** above their 2024 average.

The level is not the story. The direction of travel is.

## Rent growth has slowed every month this year

| Month, 2026 | Index | Rise on the month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January | 112.96 | |
| February | 113.65 | +0.69 |
| March | 114.20 | +0.55 |
| April | 114.62 | +0.42 |
| May | 114.97 | +0.35 |
| June | 115.22 | +0.25 |
| July | 115.45 | +0.23 |

Every month in 2026 has added less than the month before it. Not once has the
increment gone back up.

Rents are still rising. They are rising more slowly every month, and the deceleration
has been uninterrupted for seven months.

## New lets and renewals have separated

This is the part almost nobody reports, and it is the most useful thing in the data.

The index splits into rents agreed on **new** tenancies and rents agreed on
**renewals**. They have stopped moving together.

| Series | Peak | Latest, July 2026 | Off peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Apartments, new lets | 117.48 in May 2026 | 117.30 | −0.15% |
| Apartments, renewals | 114.31 in July 2026 | 114.31 | at peak |
| Villas, new lets | 112.88 in April 2026 | 112.55 | −0.29% |
| Villas, renewals | 117.13 in July 2026 | 117.13 | at peak |

**New-let rents have peaked and started falling. Renewal rents are still at their
highest recorded level.**

Villa new lets peaked in April and have fallen in three of the four months since.
Apartment new lets peaked in May. Meanwhile both renewal series set a new high in
July.

## Why that gap matters

New lets are what the market will pay today. Renewals are what sitting tenants
agreed to before, adjusted within the limits the index allows.

New lets move first because they are priced against current demand. Renewals follow
later, because they are anchored to an existing contract and constrained by the
rules on increases.

So a gap opening between them, with new lets softening while renewals still climb,
is what a turning rental market looks like early. It is not proof of one. Two to
four months of movement in a monthly index is a signal, not a conclusion, and it
could reverse in the next release.

What it does mean, concretely:

**If you are renewing**, it is worth checking what the same building is actually
letting for now rather than accepting an increase on the assumption that everything
is still rising. The gap between what a new tenant pays and what you are being asked
is narrower than it was, and in villas it has inverted.

**If you are letting a property out**, asking rents that were achievable in spring
may not be now. Rents rose 8.8% in 2025 against 9.8% on prices, so yields have been
compressing even while both were climbing.

## What this index is and is not

- It measures **agreed rents**, not asking prices. Asking prices move first.
- The base is **2024 = 100**, which is a different base from the property price
  index. The two are not directly comparable.
- Monthly data is **noisy**. One month is nothing. Seven consecutive months of
  deceleration is worth noticing.
- It is Dubai-wide. Your community may be doing something else entirely.

## Method

Dubai Statistics Center, Residential Rent Price Index, base year 2024. Latest
refresh 7 September 2026. Figures derived by a script kept with this site.

Updated monthly.
